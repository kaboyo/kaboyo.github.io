The tomato seed microbiome is mainly shaped by host genotype and production site

Across 100 tomato genotypes from 12 sites in China, host genotype explains most of the variation in the seed microbiome.

Citation. Chen, X.*†, Olimi, E.†, Lobato, C., Berg, G., & Cernava, T.* (2026). The tomato seed microbiome is mainly shaped by host genotype and production site. mSystems, 11(1), e01441-25. doi:10.1128/msystems.01441-25

Expedito Olimi’s role. Co-first author.

Summary

How strongly the plant genotype shapes the seed microbiome has been unclear. This study compared seeds of 100 tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.) genotypes produced at 12 geographical locations in China, with all seeds processed in the same way. Plant genotype was the main factor shaping microbiome structure, followed by geographic location and the insect resistance of the cultivar. Only a small core microbiome was shared by all genotypes. Machine-learning models could predict tomato traits and production environment from seed microbiome features.

Key findings

  • Plant genotype was the primary factor shaping seed microbiome structure (R² = 0.56, P = 0.001).
  • Geographic location (R² = 0.11, P = 0.001) and insect resistance of the cultivar (R² = 0.07, P = 0.001) had smaller effects.
  • A core microbiome of 21 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) was shared across all tomato genotypes.
  • Pseudomonas, Lactobacillus, Leuconostoc and Ralstonia were found in 90% of the samples.
  • Random Forest models predicted tomato traits and production environment from seed microbiome features; core members such as Lactobacillus and Pseudomonas were connected to specific tomato traits.

Methods

Amplicon sequencing of seed microbiomes from 100 tomato genotypes and 12 production sites; variance partitioning; core microbiome analysis; Random Forest modelling.
