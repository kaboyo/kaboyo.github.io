## Summary

This review brings together recent work on the microorganisms that live in and on seeds. Seeds carry part of the plant microbiota from one generation to the next, and seed-associated microbes support plant health and resilience under adverse conditions. The review covers which microbes are found in seeds across plant species, how they are transmitted, and what they do during germination, seedling establishment, growth and stress. It also sets out the methodological challenges and the questions that remain open.

## Key findings

- Seeds are an important vector for the transgenerational transfer of the plant microbiota.
- Seed-associated microbes have documented roles in germination, seedling establishment, growth promotion and stress resistance.
- Open questions concern how the seed microbiome assembles, how it is distributed between seed compartments, and how knowledge can be translated into applications.
- Mechanistic studies are needed to move the field towards more sustainable plant production and food security under a changing climate.
