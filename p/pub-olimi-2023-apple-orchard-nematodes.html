Bioinoculants and organic soil amendments affect nematode diversity in apple orchards

In two apple orchards, sampling season influenced nematode communities more than bioinoculant and organic amendment treatments.

Citation. Olimi, E.*, Bickel, S., Wicaksono, W. A., Kusstatscher, P., Coyne, D., Weber, B., Cernava, T., & Berg, G. (2023). Bioinoculants and organic soil amendments affect nematode diversity in apple orchards. Applied Soil Ecology, 190, 105004. doi:10.1016/j.apsoil.2023.105004

Expedito Olimi’s role. First and corresponding author.

Summary

Nematodes have versatile lifestyles and are useful indicators of the condition of agroecosystems, but little is known about how bioinoculants and organic soil amendments affect them. This study followed nematode communities in two apple orchards, one under organic and one under integrated farming, after soil treatment with products containing arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, bioeffectors and organic amendments. Sampling time had a larger effect than treatment. The treatments reduced nematode richness, to different degrees.

Key findings

  • Nematode richness was higher overall in the organic than in the integrated orchard.
  • Sampling time had a significant effect, more than treatment; richness was higher in spring than in autumn.
  • Community composition was explained mainly by sampling time, followed by treatment and orchard type.
  • All treatments reduced nematode richness, with effects that varied between treatments.
  • In spring there were more bacterivorous and fewer herbivorous nematodes; the effect was driven by a few families, namely Rhabditidae and Tylenchidae.

Methods

Field trials in an organic and an integrated apple orchard; soil treatments with arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, bioeffectors and organic amendments; nematode community analysis by family and trophic guild.

Keywords

soil nematodes, nematode diversity, bioinoculants, organic amendments, apple orchards, arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi
