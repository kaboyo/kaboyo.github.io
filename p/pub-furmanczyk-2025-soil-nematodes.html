An analysis of soil nematode communities across diverse horticultural cropping systems

Soil nematode communities in fifteen European horticultural fields differ by cropping system and management.

Citation. Furmanczyk, E. M.*, Kozacki, D., Ourry, M., Bickel, S., Olimi, E., Masquelier, S., Turci, S., Bohr, A., Maisel, H., D’Avino, L., & Malusà, E.* (2025). An analysis of soil nematode communities across diverse horticultural cropping systems. Soil Systems, 9(3), 77. doi:10.3390/soilsystems9030077

Expedito Olimi’s role. Co-author.

Summary

Soil nematode communities give information on soil quality and health under different cropping systems and management practices. This study evaluated nematode communities and the abundance of trophic groups in fifteen fields with different cropping systems, managed organically or conventionally, across several European climatic zones. Nematode densities differed significantly between cropping systems and management types and were related to the duration of the cropping system. The ecological indices indicated disturbed soil conditions in all three cropping systems.

Key findings

  • Population densities ranged from 121 to 799 individuals per sample.
  • Density was lowest in the vegetable cropping system (about 300 individuals on average) and highest in the long-term fruiting system (more than 500 on average).
  • The occurrence and abundance of bacterial, fungal and plant feeders depended partly on cropping system or management.
  • 22 genera and one family (Dorylaimidae) were identified across five trophic groups; Rhabditis and Cephalobus occurred in all fields and samples.
  • Phosphorus content was the only soil chemical parameter positively correlated with total nematode population and the abundance of bacterial feeders.
  • All three cropping systems were characterised by disturbed soil conditions dominated by bacterivorous nematodes.

Methods

Nematode extraction and counting from fifteen fields; trophic group assignment; taxonomic classification of a subset of samples; soil chemical analysis; nematological ecological indices.

Keywords

soil nematodes, trophic groups, horticulture, organic farming, soil health, ecological indices
