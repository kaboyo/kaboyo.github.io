Live bacterial inoculants shift the apple plant and soil microbiome more than their volatile metabolites do.

**Citation.** **Olimi, E.**\*, Duller, M., Stangl, M., Bickel, S., Battisti, A., Kusstatscher, P., Wicaksono, W. A., Abdelfattah, A., Cernava, T., & Berg, G. (2025). Plant microbiome responses to bioinoculants and volatiles. *Environmental Microbiome*, 20, 55. [doi:10.1186/s40793-025-00715-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40793-025-00715-4)

**Expedito Olimi's role.** First and corresponding author.

## Summary

Biological solutions are increasingly adopted in plant production, but their effect on native soil and plant microbiomes needs to be understood in detail. This study compared bacterial inoculants and volatile compounds, applied alone and in combination, on the apple plant and soil microbiome. Specially designed microcosms kept the plant compartments separate. Treatment effects depended on the compartment and the soil. Live bacteria had a stronger effect than their volatiles, particularly on the root microbial community.

## Key findings

- Treatment effects on the native microbiome were specific to plant compartment and soil.
- Live bacterial inoculants had a stronger effect on the plant and soil microbiome than volatiles (R² = 5% vs. 3%), especially in roots.
- Some effects were treatment-specific: 2-butanone influenced bacterial diversity in the phyllosphere, and fungal richness increased in *Serratia*-treated soils.
- The results bear on the merits of applying living microorganisms and support microbiome management as a way to enhance microbiota functions.

## Methods

Microcosm experiment with apple plants separating plant compartments; bacterial inoculants and volatile compounds as single and combined treatments; microbiome profiling of plant and soil.
