Insights into the microbiome assembly during different growth stages and storage of strawberry plants

Tracking the strawberry microbiome through growth stages and storage shows that plant compartment, not cultivar, drives assembly.

Citation. Olimi, E.*, Kusstatscher, P., Wicaksono, W. A., Abdelfattah, A., Cernava, T., & Berg, G. (2022). Insights into the microbiome assembly during different growth stages and storage of strawberry plants. Environmental Microbiome, 17, 21. doi:10.1186/s40793-022-00415-3

Expedito Olimi’s role. First and corresponding author.

Summary

Microbiome assembly matters for plant growth and health, but the process was largely unknown for the fruit microbiome. This study tracked the microbiome of two strawberry cultivars through the growth stages and during storage. Strawberry plants carried a highly diverse and abundant microbiome. Compartment type was the main driver of microbial diversity, structure and abundance, and belowground habitats were clearly separated from the phyllosphere. Microbial transfer was highest from leaves and flowers to ripe fruits.

Key findings

  • The most abundant bacterial families were Sphingomonadaceae (25%), Pseudomonadaceae (17%) and Burkholderiaceae (11%); the most abundant fungal group was Mycosphaerella (45%).
  • All compartments carried 107 to 1010 marker gene copies per gram fresh weight; 6049 bacterial and 1501 fungal ASVs were found, with higher values belowground than in the phyllosphere.
  • Compartment type explained 45% (bacteria) and 61% (fungi) of the variation, compared with 1.6% and 2.2% for cultivar.
  • Only a low proportion of the microbiome was transferred from soil via the rhizosphere to the phyllosphere.
  • During fruit development, transfer was highest from leaves and flowers to ripe fruits, where most bacteria occurred inside the pulp.
  • In postharvest fruits diversity decreased while abundance increased; decay caused by Botrytis cinerea also decreased diversity and reduced potentially beneficial taxa.

Methods

Amplicon sequencing, qPCR and microscopy of plant compartments from two strawberry cultivars across growth stages and storage.
