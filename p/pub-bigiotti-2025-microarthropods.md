Comparison of ecological indices for soil microarthropod diversity at 17 European sites, with a new index, QBS-ar_BF.

**Citation.** Bigiotti, G., Vitali, F., Mocali, S., L'Abate, G., Malusà, E., Kozacki, D., Bertoncelj, I., Ourry, M., Pugliese, M., Maisel, H., **Olimi, E.**, et al. (2025). Estimating microarthropod diversity in cropping systems by comparing ecological indices across Europe. *Applied Soil Ecology*, 106357. [doi:10.1016/j.apsoil.2025.106357](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apsoil.2025.106357)

**Expedito Olimi's role.** Co-author.

## Summary

Microarthropods are a key part of the soil mesofauna, and their diversity is informative about soil health. This study applied several ecological indices based on microarthropod biological forms (Hill's number, Acari:Collembola, QBS-ar, FEMI, QBS-ab) to 17 agroecosystem sites under organic or integrated management. It also proposes a new calculation of QBS-ar that considers all observed biological forms, called QBS-ar_BF. Indices based on the QBS approach separated the management systems best.

## Key findings

- Biological forms highly adapted to soil life were more frequent and abundant under organic than integrated management, and in pluriannual than annual crops.
- QBS-ar and QBS-ar_BF discriminated best between management types in cropping systems.
- The new QBS-ar_BF index had higher discrimination capacity because it includes all observed biological forms.
- The results open perspectives for monitoring frameworks that detect shifts in community structure and functional traits.

## Methods

Sampling of soil microarthropods at 17 agroecosystem sites across Europe; calculation and comparison of ecological indices.

microarthropods, soil mesofauna, QBS-ar, biodiversity indices, organic farming, integrated management
