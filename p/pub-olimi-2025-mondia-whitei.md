[doi:10.1111/1758-2229.70200](https://doi.org/10.1111/1758-2229.70200)

## Summary

*Mondia whitei* is an essential medicinal plant in African societies, but little was known about its metabolome and microbiota. This study examined the root endosphere and rhizosphere of plants from five locations in Uganda and catalogued root metabolite profiles against a literature survey of 516 sources. The root microbiota was abundant and showed a strong location-specific microbial and metabolite fingerprint. A large share of the root microbiome was potentially transferred from the rhizosphere, and several root compounds correlated strongly with the root microbiota.

## Key findings

- Roots were inhabited by 50 to 500 ASVs, with an average abundance of 10^11^ gene (16S rRNA or ITS) copies per gram.
- The microbiota was dominated by Gammaproteobacteria, Alphaproteobacteria and Actinobacteria, and by the fungal groups Sordariomycetes, Eurotiomycetes and Agaricomycetes, among others.
- 45% to 70% of the root microbiome was potentially transferred from the rhizosphere into the roots.
- Among 86 compounds found in roots, fraxin, 4-methoxy-benzaldehyde, monobutyl phthalate, 2-hydroxy-4-methoxybenzaldehyde and scopoletin were strongly correlated with the root microbiota.
- These compounds have been shown elsewhere to mediate interactions with microbes, plants and animals.

## Methods

High-throughput amplicon sequencing, qPCR, multifactorial modelling, GC/LC-MS metabolite profiling, literature survey.
