To defend or to attack? Antagonistic interactions between Serratia plymuthica and fungal plant pathogens, a species-specific volatile dialogue

Serratia plymuthica HRO-C48 switches between attack and defence modes depending on which fungal pathogen’s volatiles it meets.

Citation. Rybakova, D.*, Müller, H., Olimi, E., Schaefer, A., Cernava, T., & Berg, G.* (2022). To defend or to attack? Antagonistic interactions between Serratia plymuthica and fungal plant pathogens, a species-specific volatile dialogue. Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems, 6, 1020634. doi:10.3389/fsufs.2022.1020634

Expedito Olimi’s role. Co-author.

Summary

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) are part of how microbes communicate and antagonise each other, and they offer a basis for biological control of plant pathogens. This study examined the interactions between the bacterial antagonist Serratia plymuthica HRO-C48 and three fungal plant pathogens: Rhizoctonia solani, Leptosphaeria maculans and Verticillium longisporum. Exposure to fungal VOCs changed bacterial expression of genes for stress response, biofilm formation and antimicrobial VOC production. The outcome was specific to each pair of species.

Key findings

  • Inhibition of fungal growth by Serratia VOCs differed significantly between the three pathogens.
  • In attack mode, HRO-C48 significantly inhibited R. solani while boosting its own metabolism.
  • VOCs from L. maculans and V. longisporum induced a defence mode in which bacterial metabolism was downregulated.
  • The bacterial VOC methyl acetate slightly reduced growth of L. maculans.
  • VOCs contribute substantially to species-specific interactions between pathogens and their natural antagonists and deserve more consideration in pathogen control.

Methods

Pairwise dual culture assays; bacterial transcriptomics; volatilome analysis of both partners.
