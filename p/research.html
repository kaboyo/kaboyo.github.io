Research

I want to understand how plant genetics and soil conditions together determine root colonisation and microbial assembly, and to turn that understanding into microbial solutions for sustainable plant production.

Host genotype and production site shape the seed and plant microbiome

Across a panel of 100 tomato cultivars grown at different production sites, we showed that both plant genotype and site shape the seed microbiota (mSystems, 2026). The same host signal recurs in indigenous Nigerian vegetables (J. Sustain. Agric. Environ., 2025) and in Cucurbita pepo cultivars (Front. Plant Sci., 2021), and we reviewed the field in Microbiome (2026). Seeds are a heritable and selectable route for transmitting microbes.

Growth stage, storage and disease shape the microbiome of a fresh food plant

Following strawberry plants from growth to post-harvest storage, I showed that the microbiome shifts across plant compartments and growth stages, and that cold storage and disease alter the fruit microbiome (Environmental Microbiome, 2022; among the ten most-cited articles in the journal).

Microbial inoculants and their metabolites act on plant, soil and nematode communities

In microcosm experiments, I showed that living bacterial inoculants reshape plant and soil microbiomes more strongly than their volatiles alone (Environmental Microbiome, 2025), and in apple orchards that bioinoculants and organic amendments change soil nematode communities (Applied Soil Ecology, 2023). I also contributed the transcriptomic analysis showing how Serratia plymuthica uses biofilm formation and antimicrobial volatiles against soil-borne fungal pathogens (Front. Sustain. Food Syst., 2022).

Current project: MoToMi

MoToMi is my Erwin Schrödinger Fellowship project (FWF, 10.55776/J4753), with an abroad phase at the University of Southampton (2024 to 2025) and a return phase at TU Graz (from 2026). It addresses the host genetic and chemical drivers of tomato seed microbiome assembly, strain isolation and synthetic community design, and the Microbiome (M) gene framework and its application in nematode protection.

Resources from this work include:

  • seed microbiome and metabolic profiles of 100 tomato cultivars;
  • proof-of-principle datasets on the M gene framework and its application in nematode protection;
  • a culture collection of approximately 300 bacterial isolates from tomato.

Methods

16S/ITS/18S amplicon sequencing, shotgun metagenomics, RNA-seq, untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics, qPCR and qRT-PCR, FISH and confocal microscopy; selective isolation and synthetic community design; in planta nematode infection assays, nematode community profiling, nematicidal bioassays; CRISPR-Cas9 functional genomics. Data analysis in QIIME2/DADA2, R (phyloseq, vegan, DESeq2), Python and Linux/Bash, with reproducible FAIR-aligned pipelines archived in ENA, NCBI, MetaboLights and GitHub.
