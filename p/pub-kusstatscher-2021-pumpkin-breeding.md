Across a full Styrian oil pumpkin breeding pedigree, bacterial signatures pass from seed to seed while fungi are shaped by soil.

**Citation.** Kusstatscher, P.\*, Adam, E., Wicaksono, W. A., Bernhart, M., **Olimi, E.**, Müller, H., & Berg, G. (2021). Microbiome-assisted breeding to understand cultivar-dependent assembly in *Cucurbita pepo*. *Frontiers in Plant Science*, 12, 642027. [doi:10.3389/fpls.2021.642027](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2021.642027)

**Expedito Olimi's role.** Co-author.

## Summary

Long-term plant breeding shapes not only plant characteristics but also the plant-associated microbiota. This study investigated a microbiome-integrative breeding approach in the Styrian oil pumpkin (*Cucurbita pepo* L. subsp. *pepo* var. *styriaca* Greb.). It analysed the seed and rhizosphere microbiomes of six genotypes, the complete pedigree of a three-way cross-hybrid, together with the progeny seeds. Seeds carried lower microbial diversity than rhizosphere and soil, and genotypes differed more in their seed microbiomes than in their rhizospheres.

## Key findings

- Seeds had significantly lower microbial diversity than rhizosphere and soil, and a different composition with a high fraction of Enterobacteriaceae (40–83%).
- Potentially plant-beneficial bacterial taxa, including Bacillaceae, Burkholderiaceae and Pseudomonadaceae, were enriched in progeny seeds.
- Differences between genotypes were more substantial in seed microbiomes than in the rhizosphere.
- Rhizosphere communities were assembled mostly from soil.
- Bacterial signatures were mainly linked from seed to seed, while fungal communities were shaped by soil and rhizosphere.

## Methods

High-throughput amplicon sequencing of the 16S rRNA and ITS1 genes in seeds, rhizosphere, soil and progeny seeds of six genotypes.
