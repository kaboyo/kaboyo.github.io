Polylactic acid microplastics and lead alter rhizosphere bacteria and viruses of buckwheat; viral genes may contribute to plastic degradation.

**Citation.** Wei, X., Bashir, K., Tian, X., Farooq, A., **Olimi, E.**, et al. (2026). Microplastic and lead shift microbiomes enriching viral auxiliary metabolic genes for potential polylactic acid degradation. *Communications Biology*. [doi:10.1038/s42003-026-10162-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10162-7)

**Expedito Olimi's role.** Co-author.

## Summary

Biodegradable microplastics and heavy metals increasingly occur together in soils. This study tested how polylactic acid (PLA) microplastics, lead (Pb) and their combination affect buckwheat and the bacterial and viral communities in its rhizosphere. Co-contamination lowered soil pH and nutrient availability, increased lead accumulation in the plant and suppressed growth. The viral community carried more auxiliary metabolic genes for carbon utilisation, including carbohydrate esterases that can hydrolyse the ester bonds of PLA.

## Key findings

- Combined PLA and Pb contamination reduced soil pH and nutrient availability and suppressed buckwheat growth.
- Bacterial and viral communities both changed under the treatments that contained Pb.
- Bacterial genes for carbon and phosphorus metabolism were suppressed, while viral auxiliary metabolic genes for carbon utilisation were enriched.
- A putative virus-associated carbohydrate esterase gene (P9222_28545) was identified, and its esterase activity was confirmed by heterologous expression in *E. coli*.
- The results point to a potential role of soil viruses in microplastic degradation.

## Methods

Plant experiment with buckwheat under PLA, Pb and combined treatments; soil chemistry; rhizosphere metagenomics of bacteria and viruses; heterologous gene expression in *E. coli*.
