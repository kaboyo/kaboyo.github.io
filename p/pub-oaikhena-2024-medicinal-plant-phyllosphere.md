[doi:10.1038/s41598-024-68001-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-68001-w)

## Summary

The microbiota of medicinal plants can be highly specific and can contribute to medicinal activity, yet most plant species have not been studied. This study investigated the phyllosphere of two common Nigerian medicinal plants, *Euphorbia lateriflora* and *Ficus thonningii*, combining analyses of metagenomic DNA with cultured isolates. All leaf samples were densely colonised. The two plants did not differ significantly in microbial abundance but had distinct microbiota compositions.

## Key findings

- Leaves carried up to 10^8^ microbial marker gene copies per gram, with bacteria and fungi more abundant than archaea.
- Only seven of the 27 genera isolated were found on both plants, among them dominant *Sphingomonas* spp.
- The dominant fungal families on both plants were Cladosporiaceae, Mycosphaerellaceae and Trichosphaeriaceae.
- 225 plant-specific isolates were identified; 29 are likely previously unknown species, 14 of them in Burkholderiales.
- 56% of isolates from *E. lateriflora* and 40% from *F. thonningii* were characterised as *Escherichia coli*.
- Growth of most bacterial isolates was not influenced by extractable plant secondary metabolites.

## Methods

qPCR with marker gene primers; amplicon sequencing of 16S rRNA and ITS genes; isolation and identification of bacteria; growth tests with plant extracts.
