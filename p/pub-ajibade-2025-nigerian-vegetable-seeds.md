Seeds of five indigenous Nigerian vegetables carry abundant, plant species-specific bacterial and fungal communities.

**Citation.** Ajibade, O.†, **Olimi, E.**†, Wicaksono, W. A., Oladipo, E. K., Oloke, J. K., Oyawoye, O. M., Cernava, T., & Berg, G. (2025). Indigenous Nigerian vegetables harbour plant species-specific seed microbial signatures for bacteria and fungi. *Journal of Sustainable Agriculture and Environment*, 4(4), e70102. [doi:10.1002/sae2.70102](https://doi.org/10.1002/sae2.70102)

**Expedito Olimi's role.** Co-first author.

## Summary

Little is known about the seed microbiomes of Africa's indigenous vegetables. This study profiled the seed microbiome of five economically important vegetable crops in Nigeria that had not been studied before: *Amaranthus hybridus*, *Solanum macrocarpon*, *Corchorus olitorius*, *Celosia argentea* and *Telfairia occidentalis*. The seeds harboured diverse and abundant microbial communities. Bacterial and fungal communities were specific to each plant species, while the less abundant archaea did not separate by plant species.

## Key findings

- The seeds contained 2877 bacterial, 765 fungal and 56 archaeal amplicon sequence variants (ASVs).
- Abundance was high: 10^8^ to 10^11^ gene copies for bacteria and 10^9^ to 10^11^ for fungi.
- Bacterial and fungal communities showed plant species-specific signatures; archaeal communities did not.
- The core seed microbiota included the archaeal genus *Methanobrevibacter*, bacterial genera such as *Methylobacterium*, *Bacillus*, *Paenibacillus*, *Pseudomonas*, *Serratia* and *Pantoea*, and the fungal genera *Aspergillus* and *Fusarium*.
- Random forest classification could resolve plant species from seed microbiome profiles.
- The results provide a basis for conserving these plant genetic resources.

## Methods

Domain-specific amplicon sequencing of bacteria, fungi and archaea; quantification of gene copy numbers; core microbiome analysis; random forest classification.

## Keywords

seed microbiome, indigenous vegetables, Nigeria, sub-Saharan Africa, vertical transmission, *Methanobrevibacter*
