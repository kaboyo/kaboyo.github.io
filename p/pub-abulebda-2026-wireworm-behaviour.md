## Summary

This study tested how two wireworm species, *Agriotes lineatus* and *A. litigiosus*, respond to three kinds of treatment: compost teas (numbers 16 and 19), synthetic 3-octanone, and the entomopathogenic fungus *Metarhizium brunneum* (strain 1868). Behaviour was measured under controlled conditions in rhizotrons and olfactometers. The responses were consistent across both experimental systems, which suggests that wireworm behaviour is steered by chemical cues below ground and depends on where the cue comes from.

## Key findings

- Compost tea 16 acted as an attractant; compost tea 19 had no significant effect.
- 3-octanone and *M. brunneum* were consistently repellent to both species.
- The treatments are candidates for sustainable pest management, including push-pull approaches.
- Field validation is still required.

## Methods

Behavioural assays in rhizotrons and olfactometers under controlled conditions.
