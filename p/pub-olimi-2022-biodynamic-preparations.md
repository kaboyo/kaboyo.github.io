Microbial communities of biodynamic manures, plant preparations and composts are distinct and site-specific; extracts shift individual taxa in the apple rhizosphere.

**Citation.** **Olimi, E.**\*, Bickel, S., Wicaksono, W. A., Kusstatscher, P., Matzer, R., Cernava, T., & Berg, G. (2022). Deciphering the microbial composition of biodynamic preparations and their effects on the apple rhizosphere microbiome. *Frontiers in Soil Science*, 2, 1020869. [doi:10.3389/fsoil.2022.1020869](https://doi.org/10.3389/fsoil.2022.1020869)

**Expedito Olimi's role.** First and corresponding author.

## Summary

Microbial transplants are one strategy for restoring beneficial soil traits, but little was known about the microbiota of the traditional inoculants used in biodynamic agriculture. This study characterised the microbial communities of composts, biodynamic manures and plant preparations from Austria and France, and tested extracts of biodynamic manure and compost on the rhizosphere microbiome of apple trees. The preparations differed in microbial abundance, composition and diversity and were specific to each production site, but all contained potentially plant-beneficial bacterial genera.

## Key findings

- Microbial abundances ranged between 10^10^ and 10^11^ bacterial 16S rRNA gene copies and 10^9^ and 10^11^ fungal ITS gene copies.
- Bacterial diversity was significantly higher in biodynamic manures than in compost; fungal abundance was higher in biodynamic manures, with no significant difference in fungal diversity.
- Communities of biodynamic manures and plant preparations were specific to each production site.
- In apple orchards, the effects of the extracts on overall microbial abundance in the rhizosphere were not significant.
- One to four months after application individual taxa changed in abundance, including a reduction of the pathogenic fungus *Alternaria* and enrichment of potentially beneficial genera such as *Pseudomonas*.

## Methods

Amplicon sequencing and quantitative PCR of composts, biodynamic manures and plant preparations; field application of extracts in apple orchards with rhizosphere sampling.

## Keywords

biodynamic preparations, compost, soil microbial communities, apple rhizosphere, *Alternaria*, *Pseudomonas*
